# TORTILLA TRUCK — NETWORKING STRATEGY
## The Early Play: Fresh Tortillas as a B2B Wedge

**Owner:** Emily Springerton
**Updated:** 2026-06-27

---

## The Play

The tortilla truck at retail (singles, stacks at Store 0) is good. The tortilla truck as a B2B wholesale supplier to local restaurants is better. Fresh tortillas, made daily, delivered to Pontiac and Oakland County restaurants that currently buy from a sysco-style distributor.

Restaurant-grade fresh tortillas are a significantly better product than what most distributors carry. The restaurant knows this. The restaurant just doesn't have a local supplier. STINKIES becomes that supplier.

This requires networking. Emily Prime can research targets. Emily (human) has to walk in the door.

---

## The Market

Pontiac, Michigan and surrounding Oakland County (Auburn Hills, Waterford, Clarkston, Bloomfield Hills, Royal Oak corridor) has:
- Dozens of Mexican and Tex-Mex restaurants buying tortillas by the case weekly
- Catering operations looking for local/fresh positioning
- Event venues (CARTEL + competitors) needing event-night food supply
- Institutional buyers: churches, community centers, schools

Most are buying from a regional food distributor. The tortillas arrive in a bag, shelf-stable, not made today. STINKIES makes them today.

---

## Networking Targets

### Tier 1 — Direct Restaurant Supply (Highest Priority)

**Who:** Independent Mexican and Tex-Mex restaurants in Pontiac and adjacent cities. Not chains. Independents who care about their tortillas.

**The pitch:** Fresh corn tortillas, made daily on our truck. Delivered or available for pickup at Store 0. No distributor middleman. You know what you're getting and who made it.

**Pricing:** Restaurant wholesale at $0.20–0.25/tortilla (vs. $0.50 retail). Case pricing (500+ tortillas) at $0.18/unit. Margin still strong; volume is the point.

**Volume target:** 5 restaurant accounts × 500 tortillas/week each = 2,500 wholesale tortillas/week = $500/week wholesale = $26,000/year from 5 accounts. That's before retail.

**Emily Prime action:** `supply_chain_research` query — "Mexican restaurant Pontiac Michigan Oakland County independent" — compile target list, addresses, estimated size. Emily (human) walks in.

**Emily (human) action:** Walk in during prep hours (9–11 AM). Talk to the chef or owner, not the front of house. Leave a bag of tortillas. Come back Tuesday.

---

### Tier 2 — Event Catering + Promoters

**Who:** Event promoters, venue operators, wedding caterers, corporate event planners in the Detroit/Oakland County market.

**The play:** STINKIES tortilla truck at events. Revenue per event: 300–500 tortillas at $0.50 = $150–$250 net in 3–4 hours. Plus brand exposure.

**CARTEL connection:** CARTEL is the first event account. Every CARTEL night, the tortilla truck is there for late-night. That's 3 nights/week × 50 weeks = 150 events/year at home.

**Networking approach:** Emily (human) goes to other venues, other events, introduces herself. "We have a tortilla truck. Here's what we charge. Here's what you get."

**Target:** 2 external events/week beyond CARTEL.

---

### Tier 3 — Pontiac Community Anchors

**Who:** Churches, community centers, Pontiac public schools, Lighthouse of Oakland County, Pontiac Rescue Mission.

**The play:** THE FEED is already running. These organizations know who in Pontiac needs food. STINKIES builds those relationships — not as charity marketing, but because these are the people who know the city.

**Why this matters for the tortilla play:** Community anchor relationships create word of mouth that no ad budget replicates. If the Pontiac Rescue Mission knows that STINKIES feeds people and makes the best tortilla in the city, every person who walks through their door eventually walks through ours.

**Emily Prime action:** Research community anchor orgs in Pontiac. Emily (human) visits.

---

### Tier 4 — Detroit Food Media

**Who:** Hour Detroit, Detroit Free Press food desk, Eater Detroit, local food bloggers, Detroit-area food Instagram accounts.

**The pitch:** A commissary in Pontiac with a tortilla truck outside that feeds the homeless every day and sells an exclusive energy drink you can't get anywhere else.

That's a story. It writes itself. Emily (human) has to invite the right person in and let them eat a tortilla.

**Timeline:** Don't pitch before Store 0 has been open 30 days. Let the story be real before it gets told.
