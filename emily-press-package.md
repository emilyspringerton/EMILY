### Stealth register. No numbers we can't stand behind. Intrigue over specifics.

A note on this package: every claim here is either true today or deliberately withheld. Nothing asserts a benchmark, a cost, or a date we haven't earned. The posture is *we know something you don't yet* — which is only powerful if we never get caught bluffing. When in doubt, say less.

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (Investor-facing)

**We are building the machine that builds the models.**

Most companies rent intelligence. A few hire teams to make it. We are doing neither.

We've built an autonomous system that collects its own training data, trains its own models, evaluates them, finds its own weaknesses, and goes back for the data it's missing — on a loop, without waiting for us. We call her Emily.

We are not announcing benchmarks today. We are not announcing a model today. We're telling you the system exists, it runs, and it improves on its own schedule rather than ours.

What we will say:
- The data layer is real and growing, drawn from sources others overlook — including decades of the internet's own history.
- The training and evaluation loop is closed. Output feeds back into input.
- The cost structure is fundamentally different from renting access or staffing a lab.

What we won't say yet: how far it's gotten. You'll know when we want you to know.

We're raising / partnering selectively with people who understand that the interesting part of an iceberg is the part you can't see.

*Stage: early and in active development. Forward-looking by nature.*

## 3. TECHNICAL BRIEFING (AI / engineering audience)

*Structured as "what we can say / what we're holding."*

**What we can say:**

Emily is an agent system, not a single model. A coordinating agent directs specialist agents across the lifecycle: collection, preparation, training, evaluation, and improvement. Work is decomposed into loops with explicit, measurable acceptance criteria — each loop iterates until it passes or escalates.

The defining property is the closed loop. Evaluation results don't just get logged; they redirect collection. A weakness found in testing becomes a data-gathering objective. The system is built to notice what it doesn't know and go get it.

The data layer is intentionally broad and includes archived historical web data — the same page across many points in time — which exposes models to the *evolution* of information, not only its latest revision. We think temporal signal is underused by the field.

Human oversight is structural, not optional: bounded execution cycles, full logging, and explicit escalation for decisions involving policy, law, budget, or values.

**What we're holding:**

Model architecture and scale. Training corpus size. Evaluation results. Hardware. Timeline. The specific mechanisms behind the improvement loop. These will be disclosed in our own sequence, paired with results, or not at all.

**Why the silence:** A benchmark with nothing behind it is marketing. We'd rather show the work when the work speaks for itself.
